# Pile eval: z gate and fold ratio on the founder's real library (2026-10-29, lane L5)

Roll had no test set for its piles. This one runs `evals/piles.json` (32 queries: the 5 embedding piles, 16 typed
queries, 12 nonsense strings) against the founder's real 29,681 vectors, with **proxy labels** taken from metadata Roll
already stores (no hand-labelling). It answers what lane L2 needs: which per-query z gate and which fold ratio. Every
number below is from `evidence/2026-10-29-pile-eval/` (identifier-free: counts and shares only).

## Recommendation

| Constant | Value | Why (labelled queries, means) |
|---|---|---|
| z gate (`QueryScale`) | **2.0** | Keeps 30 % of the labelled matches in a median of 956 results at 32 % precision. 2.5 halves recall (18 %) for +5 points of precision; 1.5 doubles the pile (2,125) for +13 points of recall at 20 % marginal precision. Results in the band 2.0–2.5 are still 6.4× the base rate. |
| Fold ratio | **0.4** | At z ≥ 2.0 it folds 14 % of the passing true matches, and what it folds is less precise than what stays (0.23 vs 0.34). 0.5 folds 35 % of true matches and its folded part is as precise as the whole (0.30 vs 0.32): a near-random cut. 0.6 folds 68 %. |
| Today's 0.2 cosine floor | **remove** | It passes **every** vector for **every** query (29,681 / 29,681, all 32 queries): this model's text-to-image cosines sit at 0.58–0.66 on average (sd 0.018–0.038). Untrained piles have no floor in practice today. |
| "Nothing found" | **not from the z gate** | The 12 nonsense strings pass as much as real queries (z ≥ 2.0: median 738 vs 956; z ≥ 3.0: 48 vs 133). A query-level check on the **best cosine** separated them here (below). |

**Nothing found, provisionally.** Best cosine per query: nonsense 0.694–0.741, real queries 0.740–0.820 (the only real
query under 0.745 is "an animated gif", which found none of the 4 GIFs anyway). A query-level floor of about **0.75 on
the top cosine** ("show nothing if no vector reaches it") refused 12/12 nonsense strings and no real query with matches,
but the margin is under 0.01, the set is small, and the value is tied to this model revision (`file-99a7d34084f9186b-b70`)
and this library. The other signal, the cosine sd per query, overlaps (nonsense 0.018–0.025, real 0.023–0.038). For typed
queries, L3's keyword coverage should carry exact words; treat the cosine floor as a constant to re-measure with a larger
nonsense and absent-concept set before it ships. For 4 of the 12 strings about half of what passes is text images (textHeavy
0.46–0.59 at z ≥ 2.0, base 0.18): the model reads some gibberish as "an image with text".

## Method

- **Data** (private, outside the repo): the proof's database copy (`~/egcm-evidence-raw/2026-10-29/roll-proof/db/roll.sqlite`,
  opened `immutable=1`) and the vector index copied read-only from the phone with `devicectl device copy from`
  (`…/roll-proof/index/vectors.egix`; header: version 2, 256 dims, 29,681 vectors, revision `file-99a7d34084f9186b-b70`).
  Nothing was installed or launched on the phone.
- **Query vectors** on the Mac: the s6-certified b70 package (`files.json` sha256 prefix `99a7d34084f9186b`) through
  Roll's own `GemmaEmbedder.load(.file, .tokens70)` and `embed(query:)`. Its revision matched the index's exactly, and
  `VectorIndex` refuses any other. Only text functions load (peak 1.6 GB on the first run, with the Mac's compile; 0.87 GB
  after). Two runs agree to cosine 0.9999995. Mac Core ML matched the phone at cosine 1.0 in embeddinggemma-coreml s5.
- **Scores**: one `VectorIndex.dotAll` over all queries (the product L1 moves `VectorStore` to). A pile's prompts come from
  `Preset.prompts`; its score per vector is the best prompt (as `Scorer.combine`), its z the best of each prompt's own z.
  z = (cos − mean) / sd over all 29,681 vectors of that query. Preset boosts and candidate filters are left out: this is
  the typed-query view over the whole library, so the Screenshots pile isn't trivially perfect.
- **Metrics** per query: P@20 and R@200 by cosine (gates don't change the order), capped R@200 = found / min(members,
  200); per gate: results passing, precision, recall; per fold ratio: strong part = z ≥ ratio × top z.
- **Tool**: `scripts/eval` (Swift package over RollCore and EmbeddingIndex; `PileEvalKit` holds the math, tested with
  Swift Testing). `pile-eval embed` then `pile-eval score`; see `scripts/eval/Package.swift` for the command lines.

DigUp's 0.5 "never folded an expected answer" on its evals; here it folds a third of them. The fold is a second,
query-relative z cut, and precision rises only slowly with z on this model, so a gentle ratio is the honest one.

## Other findings

- **Controls behave**: "a frame from a video" and "a live photo" stay near their base rates (0.32 vs 0.17, 0.04 vs 0.07
  at z ≥ 2.0), so the labels aren't leaking through something other than the pixels.
- **Blur-like queries pull in videos** (for L4): videos are 29 % of what the Blurry pile's prompt passes at z ≥ 2.0 (base
  17 %), 43 % for "motion blur", 49 % for "an animated gif". Roll indexed 5,183 of 5,190 videos from small local previews,
  which look soft. This backs the founder's "Blurry = photos with blur < 25".
- **Embedding-blind kinds**: panoramas, Portrait mode and GIFs aren't found by their names (P@20 0). Their subtypes are
  better as filters than as prompts.
- **Private can't be graded**: `sensitive` is NULL on all 29,681 rows, so the Private pile is embedding-only (1,800 at
  z ≥ 2.0) and unmeasured.

## What proxy labels can't say

- A label is a superset or a neighbour of a query, not its answer: "a handwritten note" is graded against every utility
  image, "a chat screenshot" against every screenshot. Precision is a floor for subset queries, and recall is against the
  whole rule, so broad rules (thousands of members) can't reach high recall at any useful gate.
- `isUtility` and `blur` are Vision's and Roll's own scores, with their own errors; blur < 25 has 67 members, so its
  precision mostly measures how few they are.
- Photos of screens, printed screenshots or saved images count as wrong for "screenshot" while a person would accept them.
- One library, one model revision, one person's habits; 12 nonsense strings and no "absent concept" queries (e.g. an
  animal that isn't in the library), which is the realistic nothing-found case.
- Scores are without preset boosts and filters: in the app the Screenshots and Blurry piles are filtered first, so their
  in-app precision is higher; the gate there decides only how long the pile runs.

## Rerun

```
cd scripts/eval
ROLL_GEMMA_PATH=/abs/embeddinggemma-coreml/swift swift build -c release --product pile-eval
.build/out/Products/Release/pile-eval embed --spec ../../evals/piles.json --model <folder with b70/files.json> \
  --store <scratch model store> --vectors <private>/query-vectors.json
.build/out/Products/Release/pile-eval score --spec ../../evals/piles.json --vectors <private>/query-vectors.json \
  --index <private>/vectors.egix --db <private>/roll.sqlite --out ../../docs/investigations/evidence/<date>-pile-eval
```

(`swift build` puts products under `.build/out/Products/Release` with this toolchain, or `.build/release`.) Delete the
scratch model store afterwards (it holds a copy of the model and its compiled form).
